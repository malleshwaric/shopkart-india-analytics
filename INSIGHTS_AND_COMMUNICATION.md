# Business Insights & Stakeholder Communication
## ShopKart India — Retail & E-Commerce Analytics

---

## 1. Business Context

### Who commissioned this analysis?
The VP of Sales, Head of Category Management, and the Growth team at ShopKart India — a D2C e-commerce brand. The quarterly business review is coming up and the leadership team needs a clear picture of which categories, geographies, and customer segments are driving (and dragging) performance.

### What problem are we solving?
ShopKart has strong Electronics sales but suspects the category mix is too concentrated, margins are being eroded by discounts, and the return rate is too high. The team wants data-driven answers to:
- Which categories make the most profit (not just revenue)?
- Are our discounts driving incremental revenue or just giving away margin?
- Where are our best customers geographically, and how do we grow those markets?
- What's causing the 10.3% return rate?

### Why does it matter?
At ₹45.9Cr revenue with a 14% discount outlay (₹7.5Cr given away), optimising the discount strategy alone could recover ₹1–2Cr in annual margin. Reducing returns from 10.3% to 7% would save approximately ₹1.5Cr in reverse logistics and restocking costs.

---

## 2. Key Findings

### Finding 1: Electronics = 63.2% of revenue but NOT the best-margin category
- Electronics revenue: ₹29.0Cr | Margin: 32.1%
- Fashion revenue: ₹1.64Cr | Margin: 37.2%
- Books revenue: ₹0.43Cr | Margin: 34.6%
- Home & Kitchen: ₹8.41Cr | Margin: 31.2%

**So what:** The business is heavily dependent on Electronics — one supply chain disruption, price war, or regulatory change in that category could significantly impact revenue. Meanwhile, Fashion has the best margin at 37.2% but only 3.6% revenue share. A strategic push to grow Fashion could improve both revenue diversification and overall margin.

---

### Finding 2: Maharashtra drives 19.1% of revenue — nearly twice the second state
- Maharashtra: ₹8.79Cr (19.1%)
- Tamil Nadu: ₹5.01Cr (10.9%)
- Rajasthan: ₹4.97Cr (10.8%)
- All other states: relatively balanced

**So what:** Maharashtra's dominance likely reflects Mumbai's purchasing power and population density. But it also represents a concentration risk. If Maharashtra is disrupted (logistics, weather, economic shock), 19% of revenue is at risk. The relatively even spread in other states (all 8–11%) suggests the platform has national appeal — the opportunity is to replicate Maharashtra's penetration in Tamil Nadu and Rajasthan.

---

### Finding 3: 10.3% return rate is at the high end of acceptable for Indian e-commerce
- Total returns: 513 of 5,000 orders
- Industry benchmark: 8–12% for multi-category e-commerce
- ShopKart is in range but at the higher end

**So what:** Without return reason data, we can't determine root cause — but the most common return drivers in Indian e-commerce are: product doesn't match description, size/fit issues (Fashion), damaged in transit, and quality below expectation. Electronics returns are often the most expensive to process. A post-return survey would give the data needed to reduce this cost.

---

### Finding 4: UPI is the leading payment method at 39.2%, but COD is still 25.1%
- UPI: 1,961 orders (39.2%)
- COD: 1,254 orders (25.1%)
- Credit Card: 782 (15.6%)

**So what:** 25.1% COD is significantly higher than the 10–15% target most D2C brands aim for. COD orders have higher return rates (customers can refuse delivery), higher processing costs, and delayed cash flow. A COD-to-prepaid conversion strategy — offering ₹50–100 discount for switching to UPI — could reduce COD share to 15% within 6 months.

---

### Finding 5: Revenue peaked in August 2025 at ₹2.33Cr and has been gradually declining
- Aug 2025: ₹2.33Cr (peak)
- Dec 2025: ₹2.09Cr
- Apr 2026: ₹1.67Cr
- Jun 2026: ₹2.12Cr (recovering)

**So what:** The decline from August peak to April trough is approximately 28%. The August peak likely reflects an end-of-season sale or marketing push. The recovery in June is encouraging. The question is whether the August peak was driven by sustainable demand or one-time discounting that pulled forward future purchases.

---

### Finding 6: Premium customers (15.3% of base) drive disproportionate value
- Regular: 461 customers (46.1%)
- New: 386 (38.6%)
- Premium: 153 (15.3%)

**So what:** 153 Premium customers — 15.3% of the base — are likely driving 30–40% of Electronics revenue given the high AOV. Identifying what makes these customers premium (frequency, basket size, category) and replicating those behaviours in Regular customers is the most high-value CRM opportunity.

## 3. Business Recommendations

| Priority | Recommendation | Expected Impact |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 High | Grow Fashion category — increase SKU range and marketing budget | Fashion has highest margin (37.2%); improving share from 3.6% to 8% adds ₹2Cr at better margins |
| 🔴 High | Launch COD-to-prepaid conversion campaign — ₹75 UPI discount | Reduce COD from 25% to 15%; save ₹0.5Cr in processing costs + reduce returns |
| 🟠 Medium | Add post-return survey — collect return reason data for all categories | Understand return drivers; targeted fix could reduce returns from 10.3% to 7–8% |
| 🟠 Medium | Replicate Maharashtra playbook in Tamil Nadu and Rajasthan — both at 10.8-10.9% | These two states have national-level potential; targeted state campaigns could add ₹3–4Cr |
| 🟡 Low | Build Premium customer loyalty programme — early access, free returns, exclusive categories | Deepen engagement with the 153 high-value customers driving disproportionate revenue |
| 🟡 Low | Investigate the Aug–Apr revenue decline — separate seasonality from promotion effect | Understand whether discounting pulled forward demand or generated incremental revenue |

## 4. Stakeholder Presentation Script

### Opening (for a VP of Sales — 30 seconds)
> "I've analysed all 5,000 orders and ₹45.9 crore in revenue for the period. The headline is that we're over-concentrated in Electronics, our Fashion margin opportunity is untapped, and we're giving away ₹7.5 crore in discounts without a clear picture of incremental impact. I want to walk through four decisions we can make today based on this data."

---

### Walking through the data (15-minute Category Review)

**Slide 1 — Revenue & Margin Snapshot (2 minutes)**
> "₹45.9 crore net revenue, 5,000 orders, ₹91,884 average order value. Gross margin across the portfolio is 32.3%. That sounds healthy until you look at category mix — Electronics is 63% of revenue but only 32.1% margin. Fashion is 37.2% margin but only 3.6% revenue share. We're doing well in the category that happens to have our lowest margin. There's a structural imbalance to fix."

**Slide 2 — The Category Opportunity (3 minutes)**
> "If we grew Fashion from 3.6% to 8% of revenue — just doubling the category size — at 37.2% margin versus Electronics' 32.1%, the incremental margin improvement on ₹2Cr of Fashion revenue would be approximately ₹100K more profit than if that same revenue had come from Electronics. Scale that to ₹5Cr of Fashion revenue and you're looking at a ₹250K margin improvement just from category mix. This is the highest-leverage lever we have without changing pricing or discounting."

**Slide 3 — The Discount Problem (3 minutes)**
> "We gave away ₹7.5 crore in discounts — 14% of gross revenue. The question we can't answer from this data alone is: did those discounts generate incremental orders, or did they just reduce margin on orders that would have happened anyway? My recommendation is we run a controlled experiment next quarter — hold discounts steady on one category, increase on another, and measure conversion delta. Without that, we're flying blind on ₹7.5 crore of annual spend."

**Slide 4 — Geography (3 minutes)**
> "Maharashtra at 19% of revenue is great, but it's also a concentration risk. Tamil Nadu and Rajasthan are both at 10.8–10.9% — they have the demand base to grow significantly. What did we do in Maharashtra that drove 2× the revenue share of these states? If the answer is more marketing spend or better logistics coverage, we can replicate it. I'd like to propose a Tamil Nadu growth initiative for Q3 with a ₹50L marketing budget and the hypothesis that we can take that state from 10.9% to 15% of revenue within two quarters."

**Slide 5 — COD (4 minutes)**
> "25% COD is costing us in three ways — processing costs, return rate uplift (COD customers return more), and delayed cash flow. The good news is that UPI is already at 39% — our customers are digital. We just need to incentivise the switch. A ₹75 UPI cashback on orders above ₹2,000 would cost us roughly ₹60L annually if half our COD customers convert — but we'd save significantly more in reduced returns and processing. The math works."

---

### Handling category manager pushback

**Q: "Electronics is our volume driver — we can't de-prioritise it for Fashion."**
> "Agreed — we're not de-prioritising Electronics. We're growing Fashion alongside it. Electronics has a ceiling set by our SKU range and competition from Amazon and Flipkart. Fashion has room to grow precisely because the competitive intensity is lower in our positioning. The goal is to improve the overall margin mix, not reduce Electronics revenue."

**Q: "The return rate is 10.3% — that's within industry range. Is it really worth focusing on?"**
> "It's within range today. But we don't have return reason data — which means we might be absorbing avoidable returns. A ₹10,000 Electronics return costs us ₹500–800 in reverse logistics and 30% restocking loss. If even 20% of returns are avoidable — wrong size, description mismatch, quality photos — fixing those could save ₹1Cr+ annually. The cost of adding a return survey is essentially zero."

## 5. Interview Talking Points

### How to describe this project in an interview

**The 60-second version:**
> "I built an end-to-end e-commerce analytics project for a simulated Indian D2C brand — 1,000 customers, 5,000 orders, 15,000 line items across 7 categories. The most interesting business insight is the margin-revenue tension in the category mix: Electronics drives 63% of revenue but has the lowest margin at 32.1%, while Fashion has the best margin at 37.2% but only 3.6% revenue share. The strategic recommendation is to grow Fashion, not just optimise Electronics. I also identified that 25% COD is a cost driver — at that level it's significantly above best-practice, and a UPI incentive programme could recover the cost within two quarters."

**Key metrics to memorise:**
- ₹45.9Cr revenue, 5,000 orders, ₹91,884 AOV
- Electronics: 63.2% revenue, 32.1% margin
- Fashion: 3.6% revenue, 37.2% margin — highest margin
- 10.3% return rate
- UPI: 39.2% | COD: 25.1%
- Maharashtra: 19.1% of revenue
- ₹7.5Cr in discounts (14% of gross revenue)

**Technical questions you might be asked:**

*"How did you calculate gross margin in SQL?"*
> "I joined order_items to products on product_id to get cost_price per item. Gross profit per line = line_total minus (quantity × cost_price). Then I aggregated by category and divided total profit by total line_total to get margin percentage. The key was using line_total (which already accounts for discounts applied at order item level) rather than unit_price × quantity."

*"How would you build an RFM segmentation on this dataset?"*
> "Recency = days since last order per customer. Frequency = count of distinct orders. Monetary = sum of net_amount. I'd score each on a 1–5 scale using NTILE(5) OVER (ORDER BY metric), then combine into an RFM score. Customers with 5-5-5 are Champions; 1-1-1 are At Risk. This segments the 1,000 customers into actionable groups for targeted CRM campaigns."
